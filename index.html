What this is
La REVUELTA is an independent, bilingual research initiative working at the intersection of participatory research, narrative transformation, and peacebuilding.

We explore how individuals and communities understand and navigate experiences of peace, resistance, identity, and social change.

Our approach combines qualitative and participatory research methods, including fieldwork, interviews, community dialogues, and collaborative interpretation. Rather than imposing predetermined narratives, we work with participants to understand how experiences are represented, whose perspectives are amplified, and which stories remain overlooked.

Beyond documenting experiences, La REVUELTA explores how research can contribute to narrative shifts. By bringing forward perspectives that complicate dominant narratives of conflict, crisis, and resistance, we aim to create space for dialogue, reflection, and more nuanced understandings of communities.

Research findings are translated into accessible, bilingual publications and community-facing materials, with the potential to inform future dialogue, collaboration, and locally grounded interventions.

Approach / Methodology
La REVUELTA is built on three connected pillars: participatory inquiry, narrative transformation, and applied research translation.

Listening Labs
Who does a story belong to once it has been told?

In research, journalism, and documentary work, people are frequently invited to share their experiences. Yet once an interview ends, they may have little influence over how those experiences are interpreted, written, or shared.

Listening Labs invite participants to engage with emerging findings, question interpretations, and contribute to how their experiences are presented. Conversations are a starting point, not a finished product.

This is not a rejection of research. It is an effort to make research more accountable to the people who make it possible.

Why a zine

Research often ends in reports written for institutions, academics, or funders. Their language, format, and distribution can make them difficult to access, particularly for the communities whose experiences made the research possible.

La REVUELTA translates findings into a bilingual zine. A zine can hold interviews, oral histories, photography, artwork, participant writing, field notes, and historical context at once, so findings stay complex without becoming inaccessible. Each edition returns to the communities involved as something they can read, share, discuss, and use themselves.

Research should not only be accessible to those who study communities, but also to the people who make that research possible.

Field notes — Guatemala & Mexico
La REVUELTA began as a field journal through For The Culture NY. Selected notes from the field:
Xela, Guatemala March 2026
Resistencia, Xela
Underground scenes in Xela: live music, political tables, art, people asking hard questions about what resistance actually looks like.
Xela, Guatemala March 2026
Women's March, Xela
Was lucky to be in Xela on International Women's Day — March 8. Walked next to women and young girls from the center to the intercultural center. Signs reading "Es mi primera marcha." "Por las que lucharon y no fueron escuchadas."
Xela, Guatemala March 2026
Trama Textil
Met incredible women and their craft. So much skill, patience, and care in every step of the process — something really beautiful about sitting together, weaving side by side. Culture being kept alive, quietly, intentionally, as resistance.
San Cristóbal, Chiapas, Mexico March 2026
Street Art, San Cristóbal
Walls covered in murals and political graphics — a city that wears its voice publicly.
Oaxaca, Mexico March 2026
Grabado, Oaxaca
Printmaking is one of the most popular forms of art in Oaxaca City — you can see it everywhere. A huge connection between art and political resistance. More popular spaces exist in the center; smaller, more underground spaces exist for those who know where to look.
Oaxaca, Mexico March 2026
Feminism y Grabado, Oaxaca
Took a class on Social Movements through a local org and was connected with feminist printmakers in Oaxaca. Women here are doing powerful work — the kind that doesn't always show up in obvious ways.
